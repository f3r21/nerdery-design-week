# Vello discovery interviews — P06

| | |
|---|---|
| **Participant** | P06 |
| **Role** | Community Admin (volunteer) |
| **Context** | 63, chairs the Kestrel Park residents' association, retired teacher, resident 24 years |
| **Date** | 2026-06-18 |
| **Length** | 47 min |
| **Interviewer** | R1 |
| **Notes** | Currently moderates the association's 90-person mailing list and a recommended-trades list she maintains in a spreadsheet. |

---

**R1:** Tell me about the trades list.

**P06:** It started as a page in the newsletter, about seven years ago. Someone asked for a plumber and I put three names in, and then it just grew. Now it's a spreadsheet with, I think, forty-something names, and I send it out when someone asks.

**R1:** How does somebody get on it?

**P06:** Somebody who lives here has to have used them and said they were good. That's the rule. I don't put anyone on it because they asked me to.

**R1:** Do people ask?

**P06:** Constantly. I get emails every week from businesses wanting to be on the list, and I ignore all of them. The moment it's advertising, it's worthless.

**R1:** How do you decide when it's borderline?

**P06:** [pause] Honestly, it's a feeling. If I know the person recommending, it goes on. If I don't know them, I leave it in my drafts and think about it, and then I forget, and it doesn't go on.

**R1:** So not knowing the recommender is effectively a no.

**P06:** When you put it like that it sounds terrible. But yes. Which is a problem, because the people I don't know are almost always the ones who moved in recently. So the list is a bit of a closed shop and I'm aware of it.

**R1:** Is there anyone who's been disadvantaged by that?

**P06:** There was a young woman doing ironing, last year, lovely by all accounts, and she got on the list about four months after she should have because I didn't recognise the name of the person vouching for her. That sits badly with me.

**R1:** What would help?

**P06:** I don't know. Something that let me see, this person has been vouched for by three people, even if I don't know any of them. Three unknowns might be worth one Denise.

**R1:** How much time does the list take you?

**P06:** It's not the list, it's the emails. Probably three hours a week across everything, and it's all evenings. I'm doing this instead of watching television.

**R1:** Has it ever gone wrong?

**P06:** Twice. Once a decorator who was on the list did a bad job and the woman came to me, quite angry, as if I'd employed him. And I had to say, I'm a retired teacher with a spreadsheet, I'm not a regulator.

**R1:** How did you resolve it?

**P06:** I took him off the list. Which felt too harsh, because it was one job and he'd been fine for others, but I didn't have anything in between. Either you're on it or you're not.

**R1:** What was the second time?

**P06:** Two neighbours fell out over a fence, and both of them had used the same gardener, and somehow I ended up in the middle of it. That one I refused. I said I'm not doing this, you're grown adults.

**R1:** So there's a limit to what you'll take on.

**P06:** There has to be. People assume that because you volunteer for something you've volunteered for everything. I'll vouch for a plumber. I will not adjudicate a dispute about money between two people I have to see at the shop.

**R1:** Where does Kestrel Park end, for the purposes of the list?

**P06:** [laughs] That is the great unanswerable question. The association boundary is the old ward boundary, which nobody alive uses. As far as residents are concerned it's the park, the two roads either side, and up to the railway. But the flats at the north end, the big block, they're technically in and they've never come to a single meeting. And the estate past the park is technically out and I know four families there who'd say they live in Kestrel Park.

**R1:** Does the boundary matter in practice?

**P06:** Only when someone's excluded by it and gets upset. Otherwise nobody thinks about it.

**R1:** Do you hear from residents who aren't connected to anyone?

**P06:** Yes, and they're the ones I worry about. Older residents mostly, in the flats. They don't come to things, they're not on the mailing list, and they're the ones who get charged four hundred pounds for a job worth eighty. I hear about it afterwards from a daughter or a son.

**R1:** If a platform did this, what would you want from it?

**P06:** For it to do the boring half. The chasing, the record of who vouched for whom, the reminder that somebody's been waiting a fortnight. I'll happily keep making the judgement calls, that's the bit I'm good at. It's the admin that's eating my evenings.

**R1:** And would you want to be identified as the person who approved someone?

**P06:** [pause] Not by name to everyone, no. It's a small place. If I turn someone down I'd rather they didn't know it was me who did it.
