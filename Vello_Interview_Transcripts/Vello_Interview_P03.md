**R2:** Can you tell me about the last time you needed help with something here?

**P03:** The shower. That was March. The shower stopped, and I rang my daughter, and she sorted it out. She always sorts it out.

**R2:** How did she sort it out?

**P03:** I don't know how she does it, on the computer somehow. She rings me and says he's coming Tuesday between ten and twelve, and then he comes. That's all I need to know really.

**R2:** Do you ever arrange things yourself?

**P03:** I used to. There was a man, Ken, for years, he did everything in this flat. But he retired, or he died, I'm not actually sure which. And after Ken I didn't have anyone.

**R2:** Do you know your neighbours here?

**P03:** [pause] Not really. Not any more. There's a woman on my floor I say hello to, I don't know her name. There were people I knew when I moved in but that's all changed over, it's mostly young people now, they're out at work.

**R2:** So how many people in the building could you ask for a recommendation?

**P03:** None, I should think. I wouldn't knock on a door. You don't do that now, do you.

**R2:** Does that bother you?

**P03:** It's just how it is. It's not like where I grew up, but nowhere is. I'm not sitting here feeling sorry for myself, I've got my daughter twenty minutes away and I've got the church.

**R2:** Is the church a source for this kind of thing?

**P03:** Sometimes, yes. There's a list they do, of people who'll help. That's how I got the gardener. Someone's son.

**R2:** Tell me about the shower man in March. Was he alright?

**P03:** He was fine. Very polite. He put his shoes in a bag, which I thought was nice. But I had to sit here for two hours not knowing when he'd come, and I couldn't go out, and I don't like that. At my age you plan your day around one thing and that's your day gone.

**R2:** What would have made that better?

**P03:** Just tell me a time. Not a window, a time.

**R2:** Did you pay him?

**P03:** My daughter did that too. It goes on her card and I give her the cash. She won't take it but I give it to her.

*[Daughter joins.]*

**R2:** I was asking about how bookings usually happen.

**D:** I do all of it. Mum's not on any of the apps, she's got a phone but it's for calls. So I'll find someone, I'll book it, I'll put my number down because if they ring her mobile she won't pick up an unknown number.

**R2:** Do you use local recommendations?

**D:** I try. But I don't live here, so I'm asking in a group for an area I don't live in, which is a bit odd. Mostly I just search and read reviews and go with a gut feeling. And then I worry about it.

**R2:** What do you worry about?

**D:** Honestly? Whether they'll take advantage. Not steal anything, that's not, I mean, obviously that too. But charge her four hundred pounds for a job that costs eighty because she won't question it. That's what I worry about. She'd pay it and not tell me.

**P03:** I wouldn't.

**D:** You would, Mum.

**P03:** [laughs]

**R2:** Is there a check or a badge that would help with that?

**D:** A price would help with that. If it said what it costs before he comes. All the checking in the world doesn't stop someone quoting a stupid number on the doorstep.

**R2:** And would you want to be the one booking, or would you want your mum to?

**D:** Me. She's not going to do it, and that's fine, but every one of these apps assumes the person who needs the thing is the person holding the phone. There's no version where I'm managing it for her. I end up putting my details in and then the man turns up asking for me and Mum has to explain who she is in her own flat.

**R2:** That's useful. Anything else you'd change?

**D:** Somewhere to write it down. She's had four different people in this flat in two years and there's no record of any of it. If the boiler goes again I have no idea who came last time.
