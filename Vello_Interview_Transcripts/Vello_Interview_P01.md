**R2:** So take me back to the last time you needed help with something around the house or with the kids. What happened?

**P01:** The last time, properly? That was the dog walker. Which is ongoing, so I guess it's still happening. But the start of it was, I changed jobs in January and suddenly I'm in the office three days a week instead of one, and Biscuit is home alone from eight in the morning. So that was a problem I had to solve in about a week.

**R2:** And how did you solve it?

**P01:** I asked in the WhatsApp group. There's a Kestrel Park parents group, it's about ninety people, and honestly it's mostly people trying to give away furniture. But I asked, and two people came back with the same name. Tomás. And that was that, basically.

**R2:** That was enough? Two names?

**P01:** Two names from people I know, yes. If it had been two names from strangers it would not have been enough. It mattered that it was Priya, because Priya is, you know, Priya would not recommend someone who was going to be weird about her house.

**R2:** What was the thing you were worried about?

**P01:** Keys. It's the keys. He has a key to my house and he's in there when nobody's home. That's the whole thing. Everything else, the price, the schedule, all of that is negotiable, but somebody's got a key.

**R2:** Did anything make that easier?

**P01:** I'd seen him. That's the bit I didn't expect to matter as much as it did. He walks about six dogs on the same route and I'd seen him going past the corner shop for probably a year before I ever knew his name. So when Priya said Tomás, I knew who she meant. I could picture him. That's completely different from a photo on an app.

**R2:** Different how?

**P01:** Because he lives here. If he did something wrong, he'd still have to walk past my house every day with somebody else's dog. That's, I mean, that's not a background check, but it works better than one.

**R2:** You mentioned background checks. Would you want to see one?

**P01:** I would look at it. I'm not going to say I wouldn't look at it. But if you gave me the choice between a man with a certificate who I've never seen before and a man with no certificate who Priya has used for two years, I'm taking Priya's man. Every time. I don't think the certificate is telling me what I actually want to know.

**R2:** What do you actually want to know?

**P01:** Whether he'll turn up. [laughs] Honestly. That's the real thing. Everyone talks about safety and, I don't want to be glib about safety, but the thing that actually goes wrong is people don't show up. My cleaner before this one cancelled the morning of, three times, and the third time I had people coming for dinner. That's what ends the relationship. Not anything dramatic.

**R2:** Have you used any apps for this kind of thing?

**P01:** I used TaskRabbit once. For flat-pack, the wardrobe. It was fine, the guy was fine, but it felt like ordering a pizza. He came from somewhere across town, he did the thing, he left, I never saw him again and I don't remember his name. Which is fine for a wardrobe. I would not do that for the dog.

**R2:** So there's a line.

**P01:** There's a line. One-off thing, in and out, I don't care who it is, just be competent. Ongoing thing, someone in my house, someone with my kids, completely different question.

**R2:** You said someone with your kids. Have you hired anyone for the children?

**P01:** Ah, well. So this is the thing. Last summer I did use one of the sitter apps, and I said I'd never do that. I remember saying to my husband, I'm not having a stranger in the house with the kids. And then it was August, and camp finished, and I had a work trip, and I booked someone off an app who I had never met, based on reviews, and she was great. She was completely lovely.

**R2:** What changed?

**P01:** Nothing changed, I was just desperate. That's the honest answer. When you're stuck you'll do the thing you said you wouldn't do. But I felt bad about it the whole time. I texted my neighbour to ask her to look in, which, you know, is its own kind of verification I suppose.

**R2:** Is there a distance where local stops feeling local?

**P01:** [pause] It's not really distance, it's whether the paths cross. Priya's four blocks over and that's the same place to me. But the estate on the other side of the park, that might as well be another town, and it's closer than Priya. Nobody from here shops there or has kids at the same school. So it's not blocks, it's, I don't know what you'd call it. Overlap.

**R2:** Last thing. If a service like this existed and worked perfectly, what would it do?

**P01:** It would tell me which of these people my actual neighbours have actually used. Not stars. Stars are meaningless, everyone's four point eight. Tell me Priya used him eleven times. That's the whole product, isn't it. [laughs] Don't put that in your report, I'm sure it's more complicated than that.
