# Vello discovery interviews — P05

| | |
|---|---|
| **Participant** | P05 |
| **Role** | Provider (handyman, small repairs, some garden work) |
| **Context** | 58, lives in Kestrel Park, grew up two streets away, twenty-two years self-employed |
| **Date** | 2026-06-17 |
| **Length** | 36 min |
| **Interviewer** | R2 |
| **Notes** | Interviewed in his van outside a job. Declined to be recorded on video. |

---

**R2:** How do people find you?

**P05:** They don't find me, they know me. I've done this street, I've done, I'd say I've been inside sixty houses within half a mile. My dad had the same round before me, different trade, but same idea.

**R2:** Are you taking new work?

**P05:** Not really. I've got a waiting list, which sounds grand but it just means I'm slow. I'm booked about five weeks out.

**R2:** Five weeks. Do people wait?

**P05:** For the small stuff, no, they'll get someone off the internet. For anything they care about, they wait. I've had people wait two months for a bathroom.

**R2:** Are you online at all?

**P05:** No. No website, nothing. My number's written on the side of the van and that's done me alright.

**R2:** Has anyone ever reviewed you anywhere?

**P05:** Not that I know of. I wouldn't know how to look. Somebody said I was on one of those directory things, but I never put myself there.

**R2:** So you've built a full book with no reviews at all.

**P05:** That's how it worked before all this. You do a good job, somebody tells somebody. The review is that I'm still here.

**R2:** What makes people recommend you, do you think?

**P05:** Turning up. That's ninety per cent of it. Half the trade won't ring you back. If you say Thursday morning and you're there Thursday morning, you're already better than most, and I'm not being modest, the work's good too. But turning up is the thing.

**R2:** Has a job ever gone badly?

**P05:** Once properly. I put a shelf up and it came down, and it took a mirror with it, and that was my fault, wrong fixings for that wall. She was upset, understandably.

**R2:** How did that get resolved?

**P05:** I paid for the mirror and I did the next job for nothing. Took about ten minutes to sort out because we were stood in her kitchen. She still has me now, that was six years ago.

**R2:** If that had happened through a platform, how do you think it would have gone?

**P05:** She'd have written something online and I'd have never known why the phone stopped ringing. There'd be no conversation. That's the bit that worries me about all of it, there's no room to just say sorry and fix it.

**R2:** What would get you onto a platform?

**P05:** Not much, honestly. I'm sixty in two years. But if you asked me at thirty, when I had nothing and I was desperate, then yes, obviously. That's who it's for, isn't it. Not me.

**R2:** Do you know the other tradespeople around here?

**P05:** Most of them. There's maybe eight or nine of us who all know each other and we pass work about. If someone rings me for electrics I give them Dev's number. Dev gives me the joinery.

**R2:** So there's a referral network already.

**P05:** There's always been one. It's just in our heads and on bits of paper.

**R2:** Would you want that formalised?

**P05:** [pause] I'd want to know who I was recommending. If it's a list on an app and anyone can be on it, then my name's attached to somebody I don't know, and if they make a mess it comes back on me. That's my reputation, that took twenty years. I'm not lending it to a stranger.

**R2:** What about the verification people talk about, the checks?

**P05:** It doesn't tell you anything about whether the man can plaster. It tells you he hasn't been caught for something. Those are different questions and people mix them up.

**R2:** Do customers ever ask you for documents?

**P05:** Insurance, sometimes, for bigger jobs. Nobody's ever asked me for anything else. In twenty-two years, nobody.

**R2:** Last thing. What's the biggest annoyance in your week?

**P05:** People who message at half nine at night and expect an answer, and then message again at ten to ask if I got it. And quoting. I hate quoting. Somebody sends three photos of a wall and wants a price, and you can't price a wall off three photos, and if you say that they think you're being awkward.
