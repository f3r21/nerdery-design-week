### Theme Audits & Verification

An audit of the qualitative findings against the verbatim interview transcripts reveals key nuances, overstatements to correct, and a classic **"loud-but-shallow" theme** where stated attitudes contradict actual user behavior.

---

### 1. Audit of Theme 1: Personal Vouching & Social Proof vs. Anonymous Ratings

* **Verbatim Evidence Check**:
  * **P01**: *"If you gave me the choice between a man with a certificate who I've never seen before and a man with no certificate who Priya has used for two years, I'm taking Priya's man. Every time."*
  * **P02**: *"Denise vouched for her. That's the check."*
  * **P04**: *"Almost all of them come from each other. I've never advertised."*
  * **P05**: *"You do a good job, somebody tells somebody. The review is that I'm still here."*
  * **P06**: *"Somebody who lives here has to have used them and said they were good. That's the rule."*

* **Audit Findings & Overstatement Corrections**:
  * **Supported**: All six participants confirm that named recommendations from known peers drive hiring decisions and community spreadsheet inclusions.
  * **Overstatement to Correct**: It is **overstated** to claim that users *never* use apps, star ratings, or platform reviews.
    * P01 admitted that when she was desperate during an August scheduling gap, she booked a babysitter directly off an app based on reviews. She also used TaskRabbit for flat-pack furniture assembly.
    * P02 explicitly browses apps and reads reviews, even though he remains skeptical of inflated 5-star ratings.

### 2. Flagged Loud-but-Shallow Theme: Formal Platform Background Checks & DBS Vetting

* **The Loud Stated Demand**:
  * **P02** passionately insists on mandatory platform background checks and identity verification: *"If somebody is going to be alone with my son, I want to see a check. I want to see a DBS, I want to see references I can call, I want to see ID... That should be illegal."* He later calls formal vetting *"the gap in the market as far as I'm concerned."*

* **The Verbatim Reality Check (Why It Is Shallow)**:
  * **Attitude vs. Behavior Gap**: When P02 actually hired his cleaner Marta and gave her a key to his flat, he admitted doing zero formal checks: *"Nothing, really... Denise vouched for her. That's the check."* When asked if a platform background check would have made him feel better about Marta, he conceded: *"It wouldn't have changed anything. I already had Denise. I suppose the checks are for when you don't have a Denise."*
  * **Cross-Participant Contradictions**:
    * P01 explicitly rejects paper certificates in favor of social proof: *"I don't think the certificate is telling me what I actually want to know."*
    * P05 (a handyman) notes that official checks do not measure skill: *"It doesn't tell you anything about whether the man can plaster. It tells you he hasn't been caught for something."*
    * P04 (a house cleaner) warns that heavy onboarding background checks create weeks of friction that drive away good providers who need work immediately.
  * **Methodological Insight**: Self-reported attitudinal statements during interviews often diverge from actual behavior. While platform checks sound like a top-priority requirement in theory, in practice, users abandon them the moment personal social proof is available.

### 3. Audit of Theme 2: Locality as Social Overlap vs. Geographic Distance

* **Verbatim Evidence Check**:
  * **P01**: *"It's not really distance, it's whether the paths cross... Overlap."*
  * **P04**: *"Kestrel Park is maybe half of them... It's three different worlds, really."*
  * **P06**: *"The association boundary is the old ward boundary, which nobody alive uses."*

* **Audit Findings & Overstatement Corrections**:
  * **Supported**: Requesters and community admins define "local" by shared routines and overlapping social spheres rather than administrative ward boundaries or rigid postal codes.
  * **Overstatement to Correct**: It is **overstated** to assume that service providers must be local residents of the neighborhood they service.
    * P04 lives outside the neighborhood (interview header: 15 minutes away by bus). In her words: "I don't live in Kestrel Park either. I can't afford Kestrel Park." Asked whether clients care: "They've never asked. Not once, in nine years."
    * If a platform restricted provider listings strictly to local residents, it would choke service supply and exclude the active working class.
