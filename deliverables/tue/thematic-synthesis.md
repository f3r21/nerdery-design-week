### Ranked Qualitative Synthesis of Interview Transcripts

The seven core themes synthesized from the discovery interviews (P01–P06) are ranked below by the strength and breadth of evidence supporting them across requesters, service providers, and community administrators.

---

### Theme 1: Personal Vouching & Social Proof vs. Anonymous Ratings
**Participant Count & IDs**: Mentioned by **all 6 participants** (P01, P02, P03/D, P04, P05, P06).

* **Verbatim Quotes**:
  * **P01**: *"if you gave me the choice between a man with a certificate who I've never seen before and a man with no certificate who Priya has used for two years, I'm taking Priya's man."*
  * **P02**: *"Denise vouched for her. That's the check."*
* **What Users Said**:
  * Requesters rely on named recommendations from people they know personally rather than star ratings or generic app reviews.
  * Providers build almost all of their client base through word-of-mouth chain referrals (P04: "Almost all of them come from each other"; a newsagent card brought nothing and an app brought two jobs).
  * Community managers restrict recommended lists strictly to tradespeople vouched for by local residents.
* **What We Infer**:
  * Generic 5-star rating systems suffer from severe trust inflation (everyone is rated ~4.8), rendering them ineffective for high-trust hiring.
  * The primary value of a recommendation is the **transfer of accountability** from a known peer, which no platform badge can replace.
* **Contradicting Evidence**:
  * P02 explicitly demands formal background checks (DBS, ID, references) for childcare when no personal social connection exists.
  * P01 admitted using an app with star reviews when desperate during an unexpected scheduling gap.

---

### Theme 2: Locality as Social Overlap & Familiarity vs. Geographic Distance
**Participant Count & IDs**: Mentioned by **5 participants** (P01, P02, P04, P05, P06).

* **Verbatim Quotes**:
  * **P01**: *"It's not really distance, it's whether the paths cross."*
  * **P04**: *"They've never asked. Not once, in nine years. They care that I come on the day I said and that I'm the same person every week."*
* **What Users Said**:
  * Requesters define "local" by shared routines (schools, shops, overlapping paths) and mutual acquaintance rather than strict block radius or boundary lines.
  * Service providers frequently live outside the wealthy neighborhoods they service and travel in.
  * Official association boundaries do not align with how residents perceive their neighborhood.
* **What We Infer**:
  * Restricting platform access strictly to local resident providers would severely choke supply and exclude the actual workforce.
  * Trust is built by **repeated visual presence** and community overlap, not geographic postal codes.
* **Contradicting Evidence**:
  * P04 notes that route density matters significantly to providers; traveling 40 minutes across zones creates unpaid downtime.

---

### Theme 3: High-Stakes Access (Keys/Childcare) vs. Low-Stakes Tasks
**Participant Count & IDs**: Mentioned by **4 participants** (P01, P02, P04, P05).

* **Verbatim Quotes**:
  * **P01**: *"One-off thing, in and out, I don't care who it is, just be competent. Ongoing thing, someone in my house, someone with my kids, completely different question."*
  * **P02**: *"If somebody is going to be alone with my son, I want to see a check. I want to see a DBS, I want to see references I can call, I want to see ID."*
* **What Users Said**:
  * Requesters draw a sharp line between one-off assembly jobs (low trust) and home access or childcare (high trust).
  * Key handoff is the explicit operational milestone of established trust.
* **What We Infer**:
  * Platforms cannot treat all service categories equally; low-friction transactional onboarding works for task work, but high-stakes care requires explicit verification and social proof.
* **Contradicting Evidence**:
  * P05 points out that official background checks only prove a worker has not been caught for a crime, not whether they possess trade competence.

---

### Theme 4: Price Transparency & Protection Against Exploitation
**Participant Count & IDs**: Mentioned by **4 participants** (P02, P03/D, P04, P06).

* **Verbatim Quotes**:
  * **P02**: *"I called the first number I found on Google and paid four hundred quid and I still don't know if that was a fair price. That's the actual problem. Not safety."*
  * **D (Daughter of P03)**: *"A price would help with that. If it said what it costs before he comes."*
* **What Users Said**:
  * Requesters (and family caretakers) fear financial gouging on emergency or unquoted repairs.
  * Isolated elderly residents are particularly vulnerable to overpaying.
  * Providers dislike platforms taking perpetual commission cuts on long-term client relationships.
* **What We Infer**:
  * Anxiety around trade services is often as much about **financial vulnerability** as physical safety.
* **Contradicting Evidence**:
  * P05 highlights that tradespeople cannot accurately quote complex jobs from photos alone without risking misestimates.

---

### Theme 5: Informal Community Gatekeeping & Admin Bottlenecks
**Participant Count & IDs**: Mentioned by **3 participants** (P02, P05, P06).

* **Verbatim Quotes**:
  * **P06**: *"Which is a problem, because the people I don't know are almost always the ones who moved in recently. So the list is a bit of a closed shop and I'm aware of it."*
  * **P02**: *"Everyone tells you word of mouth, word of mouth, and it's like, yes, but I need a mouth."*
* **What Users Said**:
  * Community admins manage offline spreadsheets based on personal familiarity, creating a "closed shop" that excludes newcomers.
  * Admin volunteers are overwhelmed by manual email management but refuse to take on legal liability or dispute mediation.
* **What We Infer**:
  * Manual community moderation creates an intentional bottleneck; software can offload administrative overhead while preserving human governance.
* **Contradicting Evidence**:
  * P06 acknowledges a desire to weigh multiple unknown recommendations to break the closed-shop dynamic ("Three unknowns might be worth one Denise").

---

### Theme 6: Delegated / Proxy Care Management for Dependent Family Members
**Participant Count & IDs**: Mentioned by **2 participants** (P03, Daughter D) (supported by P06).

* **Verbatim Quotes**:
  * **D (Daughter of P03)**: *"every one of these apps assumes the person who needs the thing is the person holding the phone. There's no version where I'm managing it for her."*
  * **P03**: *"I don't know how she does it, on the computer somehow. She rings me and says he's coming Tuesday between ten and twelve, and then he comes."*
* **What Users Said**:
  * Adult children manage search, vetting, scheduling, and digital payments for elderly parents who do not use smartphones or answer unknown callers.
  * Current apps fail by assuming the account holder is the on-site recipient.
* **What We Infer**:
  * A major unaddressed user model in local services is the **two-sided requester relationship** (remote manager + local recipient).
* **Contradicting Evidence**:
  * P01 and P02 book directly for themselves without proxies.

---

### Theme 7: Provider Operational Needs – Schedule Optimization & Relationship Preservation
**Participant Count & IDs**: Mentioned by **2 participants** (P04, P05).

* **Verbatim Quotes**:
  * **P04**: *"Fill my gaps. That's it. I don't need it to replace my clients, I've got clients. I need Tuesday afternoon filled, near the other Tuesday jobs, with someone who'll rebook."*
  * **P05**: *"For the small stuff, no, they'll get someone off the internet. For anything they care about, they wait."*
* **What Users Said**:
  * Established providers use platforms only to fill specific route/time gaps, not for random one-off gigs across town.
  * Providers value direct communication to fix mistakes rather than unchallengable public bad reviews.
* **What We Infer**:
  * High-quality service providers will abandon platforms that treat them as transactional commodities or impose unfair review mechanisms.
* **Contradicting Evidence**:
  * Requesters in emergencies need immediate one-off service, creating a tension with providers who prefer recurring local clients.

### Implied Data Entities & Attributes

Based on the verified themes above, a product addressing this problem space requires the following underlying data model:

```
├── User / Profile
│   ├── user_id
│   ├── role (Requester, Provider, Delegate/Proxy, Community Admin)
│   ├── name & contact_info
│   ├── address & neighborhood_id
│   └── is_elderly_or_assisted (Boolean)
│
├── Proxy / Delegation Relationship
│   ├── relationship_id
│   ├── primary_recipient_user_id (e.g., Parent)
│   ├── managing_proxy_user_id (e.g., Adult Child)
│   ├── permissions (Booking, Payment, Messaging, On-site contact)
│   └── delivery_instructions_for_provider
│
├── Vouch / Social Recommendation
│   ├── vouch_id
│   ├── recommender_user_id
│   ├── provider_user_id
│   ├── times_used_count (e.g., "Priya used 11 times")
│   ├── relationship_type (Neighbor, Local Admin, Trade Peer)
│   └── visibility_scope (Public, Mutual Contacts Only)
│
├── Service Provider & Coverage
│   ├── provider_id
│   ├── services_offered
│   ├── primary_operating_zones (List of neighborhood IDs)
│   ├── schedule_gaps (e.g., Tuesday 14:00–17:00)
│   ├── pricing_structure (Flat fee, Hourly rate, Quote required)
│   └── lead_time_weeks
│
├── Verification & Credentials
│   ├── credential_id
│   ├── provider_id
│   ├── check_type (DBS Criminal Check, ID, Trade Insurance, Reference Calls)
│   ├── verification_status (Verified, Pending, Failed)
│   └── verified_at_timestamp
│
├── Service Booking
│   ├── booking_id
│   ├── requester_user_id
│   ├── proxy_user_id (Optional)
│   ├── provider_user_id
│   ├── job_category (One-off Task vs Ongoing/Recurring)
│   ├── scheduled_slot (Exact Time vs Arrival Window)
│   ├── upfront_quoted_price
│   └── key_access_required (Boolean)
│
└── Household Service History (Record Log)
    ├── record_id
    ├── property_address
    ├── provider_id
    ├── service_performed
    └── completion_date & final_cost
```
